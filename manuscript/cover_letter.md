Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit the manuscript entitled "Explainable Machine Learning for Predicting Mathematics Literacy and Low-Performing Students: Evidence from PISA 2022" for consideration in *Education and Information Technologies*.

This study uses public-use PISA 2022 data from 613,744 students in 80 countries and economies to compare traditional statistical models and explainable machine-learning models for predicting mathematics literacy and identifying low-performing students. The manuscript contributes to educational technology and applied educational data mining by combining predictive modeling, interpretable machine-learning analysis, digital-learning predictors, calibration and subgroup checks, and attention to the statistical structure of large-scale assessment data.

The manuscript is positioned as an original empirical study, not a review. Its education-technology contribution is the finding that ICT resources and ICT self-efficacy are consistently more model-important than simple ICT availability/use indicators after accounting for broader student, family, and school context. The manuscript explicitly interprets SHAP and permutation importance as explanations of fitted predictive models rather than causal effects.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and the author has approved the submission. The study uses publicly available, de-identified secondary data from OECD PISA 2022 and does not involve new human participant recruitment.

Thank you for considering our manuscript.
