Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit the manuscript entitled "Explainable Machine Learning for Predicting Mathematics Literacy and Low-Performing Students: Evidence from PISA 2022" for consideration in *Education and Information Technologies*.

This study uses public-use PISA 2022 data from 613,744 students in 80 countries and economies to examine how digital-learning predictors — organized through a multi-level digital divide framework — contribute to the prediction and interpretation of mathematics achievement. The manuscript integrates ecological systems theory and the digital divide framework to provide a theoretically-grounded analysis of ICT variables within a reproducible machine learning workflow.

The contribution to educational technology research is fourfold:

1. **Theoretical grounding**: The study is the first to operationalize Bronfenbrenner's ecological systems theory and van Dijk's digital divide framework within a predictive modeling context, providing a structured taxonomy for organizing ICT predictors by access, skills/confidence, usage, and outcomes dimensions.

2. **Centrality of ICT predictors**: ICT resources and ICT self-efficacy are consistently among the top model-important predictors — second only to home possessions and mathematics self-efficacy — while simpler availability/use indicators show weaker associations. This pattern is consistent with the digital divide framework's prediction that skills and confidence matter more than mere device availability.

3. **Methodological rigor**: The workflow combines systematic hyperparameter tuning via Bayesian optimization (improving RMSE from 57.61 to 54.10 and AUC from 0.890 to 0.903), a stacking ensemble model, SHAP interaction analysis, and seven robustness checks (OECD holdout, subgroup evaluation by gender/ESCS/immigrant background, complete-case sensitivity, plausible-value label stability, country fixed effects, and country-group holdout). SHAP and permutation importance are explicitly interpreted as fitted-model explanations, not causal evidence.

4. **Cross-country validation**: Country fixed-effects and country-group holdout analyses demonstrate that global predictive models have limited cross-country generalization, providing an empirical basis for context-aware model evaluation in international education research.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and does not constitute a review paper (consistent with EAIT's current policy). The study uses publicly available, de-identified secondary data from OECD PISA 2022. Analysis code and non-restricted derived outputs will be made publicly available at https://github.com/Jackxiaozhiren/pisa2022-xai-math.

This submission is aligned with EAIT's scope in educational data mining, learning analytics, and the complex relationship between information technologies and educational outcomes at scale.
