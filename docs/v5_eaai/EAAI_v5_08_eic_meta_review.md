## Decisive positive axis

If EAAI accepts model-level pre-use verification of high-impact assessment analytics as a real application, the paper offers a reproducible, design-aware audit artifact with a useful negative/uncertain result rather than a selective headline.

## Decisive negative axis

If EAAI requires an existing engineering deployment, user/institution validation, or algorithmic novelty, the paper remains at material desk-reject risk. No local text change can honestly close that gap.

## Latest-neighbor update (2026-08-25)

Two full-text Frontiers 2026 frameworks now provide closer conceptual precedents for explainable fairness-aware education/assessment auditing: one combines teacher judgment, institutional fairness calibration and early-warning ablation; the other verifies exam scores with rubric, peer-consistency and human-in-the-loop evidence. They do not duplicate the PISA plausible-value/replicate-weight estimand path, but they increase the desk-risk argument that model-level verification alone may be viewed as an audit assembly rather than an engineering application. The manuscript's explicit non-teacher/non-score-verification/non-deployment boundaries and citation update are therefore necessary; they do not remove the scope risk.
